# 如何变得自律到令人发指（中英对照）

> 作者：Dan Koe（[@thedankoe](https://x.com/thedankoe)）  
> 原文：[How to become disgustingly self-disciplined](https://x.com/thedankoe/status/2101361833940791607)  
> 翻译 Url：[https://github.com/samz406/local_wenzhang/blob/main/如何变得自律到令人发指_中英对照.md](https://github.com/samz406/local_wenzhang/blob/main/如何变得自律到令人发指_中英对照.md)

![文章封面](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HSmJalmbsAAw4iR?format=webp&name=medium)

告诉我，下面这一幕是不是很熟悉。

*Tell me if this sounds familiar.*

你想改变自己的人生。

*You want to change your life.*

而且想得要命。

*Like really bad.*

你受够了自己的处境。你受够了这副身体，受够了大脑总在和你作对，受够了每个月都为付不起账单而焦虑，也受够了担心自己最终会孤独终老。

*You’re tired of where you are. You’re tired of your physique, or how your mind is not your friend, or how you have to stress over not being able to pay the bills each month, or how you’re going to end up alone.*

终于，你攒够了动力：开始健身、开始创业、开始主动和更多人交谈——这一套你很熟悉。

*You finally gain the motivation to start in the gym, start the business, start talking to more people, you know the drill.*

时间快进两到四周，一切毫无变化。

*Fast forward 2-4 weeks and nothing has changed.*

你甚至不知道这一切是怎么发生的……回过神来，你已经回到原来的生活，半点进展都没有。

*You don’t even know how it happened... you just ended up back in your old life without making any progress.*

而且这不是第一次。你认真起来，然后掉队；再认真起来，再次掉队。十年就这样过去了——有些人甚至耗掉一辈子——却始终戒不掉那个让自己活成最糟版本的习惯。

*And this isn’t the first time. You lock in, fall off, lock in again, fall off, and 10 years go by (for some people, their entire life goes by) without breaking the habit of being your worst self.*

这感觉糟透了。

*It sucks.*

就像你压根没试着改变过。

*It’s like you never even tried to change.*

因为，说实话，你确实没有。

*Because, well, frankly, you didn’t.*

你并没有真正努力过。

*You didn’t actually try.*

那你该怎么办？

*So what do you do?*

看看那些顶尖运动员、创业者、远见者和战略家。他们似乎每天工作十六个小时都不费吹灰之力。你觉得，他们真的在苦苦挣扎吗？

*If you look at the top athletes, startup founders, visionaries, and strategists who seem to work 16 hours a day without breaking a sweat, do you think they struggle to do that?*

还是说，那恰恰就是他们想做的事？

*Or is that what they want to do?*

这对他们真的很难吗？不。事实上，像普通人那样生活，对他们来说才极其困难。只要察觉自己正退化到平庸的人生里，他们就会觉得如坠地狱。

*Is it actually hard for them? No. In fact, it’s extremely hard for them to do what the average person does. It’s a living hell for them to even sense that they’re devolving into a mediocre life.*

为什么？因为那就是他们是谁。

*Why? Because that’s who they are.*

那么，我们怎样在自己的人生里复制这一点？

*How do we replicate this in our own life?*

系好安全带。

*Buckle up.*

接下来会有些颠簸。

*It gets bumpy.*

## 厌恶现状到你别无选择，只能赢

*Become so disgusted with where you are, you have no option but to win*

![火箭发射与持续改变](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HSmJSlJaMAAvomT.jpg)

火箭在发射和加速阶段，会把总质量的 85% 到 90% 用作燃料；可一旦达到轨道速度或逃逸速度，滑行就不再需要燃料。

*Rockets use 85% to 90% of their total mass as fuel during launch and acceleration, whereas coasting requires zero fuel once orbital or escape velocity is reached.*

换句话说，让你出发的东西，并不是让你一直走下去的东西。

*In other words, what gets you started isn’t what keeps you going.*

这听起来违反直觉——人生的大多数真相都是如此——但如果你想获得持久的改变，最开始就得靠蛮力把自己硬推进去。

*It’s counterintuitive, as most of life’s truths are, but if you want sustainable change, then you have to brute-force your way into it.*

这就是第一阶段。

*That’s stage one.*

发射燃料。它可以是动力、痛苦、一次胜利、一次失败，或者一阵突然爆发的意志力——某种能把你射进一段剧烈改变期的东西。

*The launch fuel. Motivation, pain, a win, a loss, or a burst of willpower. Something that launches you into an intense period of change.*

我人生中有过很多次，使用一种我称为“战术性压力”的办法。

*There have been many times in my life when I have used what I call tactical stress.*

举个例子：我会搬进一套自己负担不起的公寓，逼着自己必须把生意做到足以支付房租，否则就得流落街头。当然，我实际上不会无家可归，但当时的我真心相信会。我这样干过不止一次，也是我找到的最有效的“业务增长黑客”。听起来很极端——重点恰恰就在这里。它并不适合所有人，只适合那些相信自己的人。

*I move into an apartment I can’t afford so I’m forced to make my business successful enough to afford it, or I go homeless, as one example. I wouldn’t go homeless of course, but I believed I would have. I’ve done that multiple times, and it’s the single greatest “business growth hack” I’ve found. It sounds extreme, and that’s the point. It’s not for everyone, it’s only for those who believe in themselves.*

大约五年前，我每隔两个月左右就会故意去一次锐舞派对，喝酒、狂欢，把它当成高度优化生活中的一次“系统故障”。我也确实喜欢这样做，那像是给身体来了一次小小的电击。第二天早晨，不出所料，我会难受得要命。我能感觉到自己在事业和健康上的进展正在倒退，而这两个目标对我很重要，所以第二天醒来时，我往往会重新燃起动力，拼尽全力向前冲。

*About 5 years ago, I would intentionally go to raves, drink, and party every 2 months or so as a form of glitch in my overly optimized lifestyle. I also enjoyed doing it. It was a nice little shock to the system. The next morning, as you would guess, I would feel horrible. I felt my progress in business and health slipping, and since those goals mattered to me, I usually woke up the next morning with renewed motivation to push as hard as I could.*

这就引出了我发现的第二强燃料：对自己绝不愿过上的那种生活，产生强烈的厌恶。几乎任何想改变的人都能利用它。宿醉后痛苦地醒来是一种办法——前提是你确实有一个目标，能让宿醉变成一件坏事——但如果你不喜欢这种方式，还有另一条路。

*That leads to the second most potent fuel I’ve found, which is a strong disgust for the life I do not want to live. That’s what almost anyone can take advantage of if they want to change. Waking up with a bad hangover is one way to do it (if you actually have a goal that makes the hangover a bad thing), but there’s another way if that’s not your cup of tea.*

接下来整整一周，我希望你做一件事：

*For the entirety of this week, here’s what I want you to do:*

观察这个社会，看看大多数人最后会落到哪里。那画面并不好看：通常是身无分文、体重超标，和一个后来逐渐憎恶的人结了婚，再被利用其困境的财务责任压得喘不过气，几乎不可能脱身。这就是普通人的人生在默认状态下会抵达的终点。感谢熵增。

*Observe society and see where most people end up. It’s not pretty. Usually broke, overweight, married to someone they grow to hate, and drowning in financial responsibilities that take advantage of their situation making it nearly impossible to get out. That’s the default end state of the average person’s life. Thanks, entropy.*

如果想上整整一周还不能让你心里燃起一把火——我每次想到这些都会——那下一个选择，就是任由自己继续堕落，直到痛苦大到你此生再也不愿体验那种生活。

*If thinking about that for a week doesn’t light a fire in your belly (it does for me every time I think about it), the next option is to simply let yourself go until the pain is so great that you never want to experience that life again.*

对很多人来说，那一步其实已经不远。问题在于，他们会麻醉痛苦，把它塞进脑海最深处，因为正视它实在太疼。

*For many people, that's not far off. The problem is that they numb the pain. They shove it in the back of their mind because it would be too painful to face.*

如果你能正视它一周，这就足够奏效。

*If you can face it for a week, that will do the trick.*

现在我们有了发射燃料，但怎样才能把它真正变成一套可持续的东西？

*But now that we have the launch fuel, how do we actually turn that into something sustainable?*

## 你不够自律，是因为你还不是那个根本不把它视为自律的人

*You aren’t disciplined because you aren’t the person who doesn’t view it as discipline.*

> 到了人类历史的这个阶段，一个人理应能够塑造出一个“自我”：它不再只是生物驱力和文化习惯共同作用的产物，而是一次有意识的、属于个人的创造。  
> ——米哈里·契克森米哈赖

> *At this point in our history it should be possible for an individual to build a self that is not simply the outcome of biological drives and cultural habits, but a conscious, personal creation.*  
> *– Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi*

我恳求你真正理解这一点。

*I’m begging you to understand this.*

你觉得埃隆·马斯克早晨醒来后，会赖在床上，为即将开始的一天感到恐惧吗？你觉得他会按五次贪睡键，幻想自己不用去工作吗？

*Do you think Elon Musk lies around in bed when he wakes up, dreading the day ahead? Do you think he hits snooze 5 times, dreaming of not having to go to work?*

你觉得阿诺德·施瓦辛格必须欺骗自己，才能健康饮食、一天训练两次吗？

*Do you think Arnold Schwarzenegger had to trick himself into eating healthy and training twice a day?*

你觉得这种级别的高绩效者，会认为做困难的事很“难”吗——按你所理解的那种“难”？还是说，他们其实热爱这一切？

*Do you think that high performers of this caliber consider it “hard” (in your sense of the word) to do hard things? Or do they love it?*

这就是他们平常会做的事，一切都是自动发生的。

*It’s just what they do. It’s automatic.*

如果我正全神贯注于一个目标，比如在达到宏量营养素目标的同时减脂，那么麦当劳的放纵餐对我毫无诱惑，只会让我觉得恶心得要命。你得把我从椅子上硬拽起来，我才可能去舔一口双层四盎司芝士牛肉堡。对我来说，吃一顿平庸的垃圾食品，反而需要极强的自制力。

*If I am laser-focused on a goal, like cutting weight while hitting my macro targets, a cheat meal at McDonald's doesn’t sound tasty to me. It sounds fucking disgusting. You would have to pry me from my chair to get me to even lick a double quarter pounder with cheese. It would take an immense amount of discipline for me to eat mediocre food.*

我知道，这听起来有些自命不凡。但对我来说，减掉十到二十磅，要比那个满脑子都在盼着下一次泡进油脂里的人容易得多。

*That sounds pretentious, I’m aware of that, but it’s going to be far easier for me to drop 10-20 pounds than it is for the person who can’t stop thinking about the next time they get to drown in grease.*

无法理解这一点的人，接着会说：“你为什么不能找点乐子？放松一点！”这是他们保护自身认同的防御机制。但他们不明白，我现在享受到的乐趣，远胜于跟你一起在十五分钟内吃光一整天的热量配额，然后陷入食物昏迷，剩下一天都像树懒一样瘫着。对，可真有意思！

*People who cannot fathom this will then say, “Why don’t you have a little fun! Lighten up!” as a defense mechanism to protect their identity, but what they do not understand is that I am having more fun now than I would be going with you to eat your entire daily caloric allowance in 15 minutes just to go into a food coma and sloth around the rest of the day. Yes, so fun!*

世界上最美妙的感受之一，就是不断追逐自己的潜能，让真实的自己和想成为的自己之间几乎没有距离。你活在心流里，像置身一场电子游戏，不断接下自己选择接受的高难度任务，并因此得到丰厚奖励。奖励未必是金钱，也可能是洞见、经验，以及现实给你的直接反馈。

*It is one of the greatest feelings in the world to be in pursuit of your potential, with little to no gap between who you are and who you want to be. You live in the flow state, a video game, taking on a string of challenging quests you choose to accept, and being rewarded amply for doing so. Maybe not in the form of money, but insight, experience, and direct feedback from reality.*

可是，改变身份认同太抽象了。

*But identity change is so abstract.*

你看不见自己的身份认同。

*You can’t see your identity.*

它不像做生意，金钱能够构成直接而迅速的反馈机制：赚不到钱，是现实在告诉你做错了；赚到钱，则是现实在告诉你，至少从收入看，你正处在应该在的位置。

*It’s not like a business where you have a direct and rapid feedback mechanism like money - if you aren’t making any, reality is telling you you’re doing something wrong, and if you are making money, reality is telling you you’re exactly where you should be revenue-wise.*

那么我们该怎么办？如何把那种不那么体面的发射燃料，变成一套可持续的系统，并最终让它成为“我本来就是这样的人”？

*So what do we do? How do we take the dirty launch fuel and turn it into a sustainable system that eventually becomes “just who I am?”*

## 给我十分钟，我会告诉你怎样成为一个全新的人

*Give me 10 minutes and I’ll show you how to become someone entirely new*

> 比起眼下取得了什么结果，你更应该关心自己正在沿着怎样的轨迹前进。  
> ——詹姆斯·克利尔

> *You should be far more concerned with your current trajectory than with your current results.*  
> *– James Clear*

从统计意义上说，你就是自己反复所做之事的总和。

*You are the statistical sum of what you repeatedly do.*

而“你所做的事”，远不只是那些由身体执行的决定。

*And what you do is more than the physical decisions you make.*

一切流经你的东西，无论进入还是输出，都在一点点累积成今天的你。

*You are the accumulated result of everything that flows through you, in both directions, input and output.*

输入＝你接触到的一切。你读过的每一本书，目光只停留一瞬的每一条帖子，被你称为朋友的每一个人，在你还来不及同意之前，父母和老师就植入你脑中的每一种信念；还有你为了掌握资讯而阅读的新闻、偶然听到的对话，以及那些被你隔着距离观察的人生。

*Input = everything you’re exposed to. Every book you read. Every post you glance at for a split second. Every person you call a friend. Every belief your parents and teachers installed before you even had a chance to consent. The news you read to stay informed. The conversations you overhear. The people whose lives you watch from a distance.*

输出＝你在这个世界上创造的一切。你的决定、行动、作品与工作；没有人看着时你做的事，以及所有人都在看时你做的事。

*Output = everything you produce in the world. Your decisions, actions, works, and work. What you do when no one is watching and what you do when everyone is.*

全部公式，就只有这些。

*That is literally the entire equation.*

输入塑造了那个感知情境、作出决策的大脑；输出塑造了那个会为你带来下一轮输入的人生。二者不断复利。

*Input shapes the mind that perceives situations and makes decisions. Output shapes the life that presents the next input. It compounds.*

要改变自己，我们就必须改变这股信息与行动之流。

*To change who we are we must change this flow of information and action.*

对于输入，规则很简单。

*For input, the rule is simple.*

精心筛选并保护你的大脑每天接触的一切。你必须把自己浸泡在与理想自我相匹配的刺激中，同时断掉旧自我的养分。过去的你必须死去。

*Curate and protect everything your mind is exposed to on a daily basis. You must immerse yourself in stimuli that match the person you want to become and starve the person you used to be. The old you must die.*

有些人看到下面的内容会想：“哦，这我早就知道了。”或者：“这不就是最基础的自我提升吗？”

*Some may see what’s below and think “Oh I already knew that.” or “That’s just basic self-help.”*

可如果你真的懂，就不会还困在现在的处境里，不是吗？一句“太基础了”，让你看不见这一切原本有多简单。你没那么特殊，也不需要什么前所未见的新奇方法才能改变人生。你需要的，是那些在人类全部历史中已经反复证明有效的东西。

*But if you actually knew it, you wouldn’t be in this situation, would you? Writing it off as “basic” keeps you from seeing how simple this all is. You are not special, and you do not need something novel to change your life. You need what’s been proven to work for the entirety of human existence.*

第一，选择你的信息饮食。

*First, choose your information diet.*

大多数人在智识上都已肥胖。凡是降低你标准、浪费你注意力的内容，一律取关、静音、退订；去关注和阅读那些能提高你标准的人与思想。社交媒体时间线和其他任何环境一样：你生活在其中，它也持续影响着你。

*Most people are intellectually obese. Unfollow, mute, and unsubscribe from anything that lowers your standards or wastes your attention. Follow and read the people and ideas that raise it. Your social timeline is an environment just like any other. You live in it, and it influences you.*

第二，选择围绕在你身边的人。

*Second, choose the people you surround yourself with.*

他们的价值观、恐惧，以及对“什么算正常、什么有可能”的假设，都会渗进你心里，无论你是否愿意承认。这是最困难的部分。你可以尝试带朋友一起改变，但要帮助一个根本不想被帮助的人，难度极高。

*Their values, fears, and assumptions about what’s normal and possible leak into you even if you think they don’t. This is the most difficult part. You can try to bring your friends along with you, but it is extremely difficult to help people who don’t want to be helped.*

第三，审问自己头脑中那些不再为你服务的信念。

*Third, interrogate your mind for beliefs that do not serve you.*

关于金钱、关于你配得上怎样的人生、关于你究竟可能取得什么成就——你对这些事情的大部分信念，在你连自己使用的语言都无法理解之前就已经形成。如果你从未意识到这些信念，它们大概率也从未改变。未经审视的东西，你无法改变。

*Most of your beliefs about money, what you deserve in this life, and what’s possible to achieve were determined before you could even understand the language you speak. If you haven’t become aware of these beliefs, then it’s unlikely that they have shifted. You cannot change what you have not examined.*

第四，把被动消费换成主动消费。

*Fourth, exchange passive for active consumption.*

飞快刷完十条帖子，和慢慢读完一本书，差别大得惊人。我敢打赌，昨天看过的社交媒体帖子，你连两条都复述不出来。但一段改变人生的 YouTube 视频、一期播客、一章书，或一篇由真正热爱自己事业的人精心写成的 Substack 文章，会留在你心里，把你推向更好的方向。

*The difference between reading ten posts fast and one book slowly is drastic. I bet you can’t tell me about even 2 of the social posts you read yesterday. But a life changing YouTube video, podcast, book chapter, or Substack article that is created with care by someone who is passionate about what they do is something that sticks with you, and influences you for the better.*

## 让成功简单到：如果失败，你自己都显得可笑

*Make it so easy succeed you’d look stupid if you fail*

我忘了提一件重要的事。

*I forgot to mention something important.*

这里进入改变的第二阶段。

*This is the second stage of change.*

第一阶段是发射。也就是借助一种不那么体面的燃料，获得最初作出改变的动力。坦白说，没有它，你大概不会真正改变自己的输入，也就无法冲破大气层，进入我们即将建立的那套可持续系统所支撑的滑行阶段。

*The first is the launch. You know, using a dirty fuel to gain the motivation to make the change in the first place. Because frankly, without it, you probably aren’t going to change your inputs, and you won’t be able to break through the atmosphere and coast with the sustainable system we are about to create.*

你的输出，也就是决定和行动，会在一个四段式反馈循环中发生：

*Your outputs, or decisions and actions, occur as a four-part feedback loop:*

环境 → 信号 → 行为 → 回报

*Environment → Signal → Behavior → Payoff*

这本质上就是源自 B. F. 斯金纳操作性条件反射的习惯回路，后来又经查尔斯·都希格的《习惯的力量》和詹姆斯·克利尔的《原子习惯》得到普及。

*This is essentially the habit loop from B.F. Skinners operant conditioning, which went on to be popularized by Charles Duhigg (The Power of Habit) and James Clear (Atomic Habits).*

提示 → 反应 → 奖励，这是教科书级的行为心理学。它确实有效。

*Cue → response → reward is textbook behavioral psychology. It works.*

第一步，让你想要的输出成为阻力最小的路径，同时让你不想要的输出变得极其麻烦。

*The first step is making the desired output the path of least resistance, and the undesired output incredibly inconvenient.*

第二步，设置一个触发器，也就是启动某项行为、把你带向目标输出的信号，最好每天都会出现。

*The second is creating a trigger, or signal that starts the behavior that leads to the desired output, preferably on a daily basis.*

第三步，把成功缩小到输出的最小可识别版本：不是完成一次高质量训练，而只是踏进健身房。

*The third is reducing success to the minimum recognizable version of the output (not a great workout, but stepping into the gym).*

第四步是奖励。每一次行为完成后，立刻给自己某种愉快的东西，直到有一天，行为本身就成了奖励。

*The fourth is the reward. Something enjoyable delivered right after the behavior, every single time, until the behavior is the reward itself.*

用例子最容易说明这个反馈循环。

*This feedback loop is best illustrated with examples:*

**目标一：在健身房增肌。**

*Goal 1 = Gaining muscle in the gym.*

首先，筛选你的输入。关注两到三个能带来鼓舞和知识的来源，让信息流在你改变的过程中持续提醒目标。取关那些兜售身材焦虑、鼓吹接受平庸的健身内容。至少让一个已经坚持训练的人进入你的生活，因为他的日常会逐渐成为你的日常。审视那些被植入你心里的信念：你怎样看待自己的身体，又怎样看待自己的能力。

*First, you curate your inputs. Follow two to three sources of inspiration and education so your feed reminds you of your goal as you make this change. Unfollow the fitness content that sells insecurity and acceptance of mediocrity. Surround yourself with at least one person who already trains, because their normal becomes your normal. Examine the beliefs installed in you about your body and what you’re capable of.*

接下来，设计你的输出。

*Then engineer your outputs.*

**环境**——前一晚就把训练服摆好，清空橱柜里的垃圾食品，提前研究第二天训练要做的动作。这样你开始时会更清楚，甚至带着一点尝试的兴奋。

*Environment - Set your clothes out the night before, remove all junk food from your cabinets, and study the exercises you’re doing in tomorrows workout so you have clarity, and maybe a bit of excitement, to try them out.*

**信号**——每天在同一时间去健身。对我来说，大约是上午十一点。如果你刚开始，就为这个时间设置闹钟，并使用一种独特的铃声，让它只代表“去健身房”。

*Signal - Go at the same time every day. For me, that time is around 11am. If you’re just starting, set an alarm for that time, and make the alarm a distinct tone that represents going to the gym.*

**行为**——先到场，只承诺自己完成最低限度：一组有强度的动作。既然已经来了，你大概率会想继续做下去。

*Behavior - Show up and only promise yourself you’ll do the minimum. One hard set. Chances are you’ll want to keep going since you’re already there.*

**奖励**——在附近的果昔店，甚至加油站买一杯蛋白奶昔。我刚开始时就是这样奖励自己。它们可能没那么健康，但从全局看，你追求的最终状态足以抵消这点小小的代价。

*Reward - Stop at the local smoothie place or even a gas station to get a protein shake. This was my reward when I first started. Sure, they’re probably not that great for you, but big picture the end state you are pursuing outweighs any minor damage it does.*
